Research

Independent research · 2026 · in progress

NeuroVestibular Ocular Sensorimotor Twin for Autonomous Training

A digital twin of balance and vision for long-duration spaceflight

In microgravity the inner ear stops being a reliable guide to balance, so the brain leans on vision. Many astronauts on long missions also develop Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS), which blurs vision. These are usually studied separately. I want to know whether balance suffers more when they overlap.

Phase 1 tests that on the ground. A VR headset simulates SANS-like blur at three levels while I measure postural sway, and I compare the sway against a risk score from Meta's TRIBE v2, which predicts how visual cortex responds to a video.

Participants
About 20 healthy adults, with a spotter and gait belt throughout
Conditions
No, mild and moderate blur; firm ground and foam; still and rotating space scenes
Measures
Sway on a Wii Balance Board plus two motion sensors
Now
Running model simulations and practicing sway analysis on the PhysioNet Human Balance Evaluation dataset (Santos & Duarte) while IRB review is pending
Funding
$7,000 research grant
Next
Finish Phase 1 by February 2027. A motion platform, eye tracking and EEG come later, only if Phase 1 shows a real effect.

Independent research · 2025

Early detection of diabetic retinopathy with deep learning

A CNN pipeline in Python and TensorFlow that screens retinal fundus photographs for diabetic retinopathy, trained on more than 3,000 images from the APTOS 2019 dataset. It covers preprocessing, augmentation, training, ROC analysis and Grad-CAM maps that show which parts of the retina drove each prediction. The aim is screening that clinics without an ophthalmologist could afford.

Qualified for the Louisiana State Science & Engineering Fair. Paper in the publishing process.

Founder and developer · 2026 · Congressional App Challenge, LA-03

LIFELINE

Disaster continuity-of-care planning for Louisiana

Hurricanes in Southwest Louisiana close pharmacies, dialysis centers and clinics, often for weeks. LIFELINE takes an address or ZIP and shows the real providers nearby, flags the ones inside FEMA flood hazard areas, checks live National Weather Service alerts, and suggests a backup for each. The plan can be printed, shared or saved on the device, and works offline once installed.

There is no invented data. Providers come from the NPI Registry, CMS Care Compare and HRSA, refreshed weekly by GitHub Actions; weather, shelters and storms come live from NWS, FEMA and the National Hurricane Center.

Open the app Source on GitHub

How it picks a backup

The nearest provider of the same type that is:

  1. not your usual provider (hospitals must list emergency services)
  2. outside FEMA high-risk flood areas
  3. at least 15 km from your usual one, so one flood is less likely to close both
  4. within 120 km of home

If nothing qualifies, rules 2 and 3 relax one at a time and the app says which.
